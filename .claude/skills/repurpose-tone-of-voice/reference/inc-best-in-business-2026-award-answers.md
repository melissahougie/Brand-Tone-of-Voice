# Approved example: Inc. 2026 Best in Business Awards submission (Software category)

Approved final answers for rePurpose's Inc. Best in Business Awards entry, submitted under the
Software category. Three of the four pieces below were drafted and refined earlier in this
project (the SB 54/product-story answer, the leadership/moat answer, and the metrics/proof
answer); the short company-pitch answer is new and wasn't reviewed against the voice guide
before approval. Full drafting history and intermediate versions live in this session's
transcript; this file captures only the final, approved text.

Note: the 2026 Inc. Best in Business Awards entry fee is $695 if submitted by 8/14, $995 if
submitted by 9/11. Not voice-relevant, kept here only because it was part of the source note.

**Why the three refined pieces work, mapped back to the guide:**

- **The SB 54/product-story answer earns its "lofty" framing.** It was revised specifically
  because early drafts read as a feature list. The fix wasn't adding enthusiasm, it was adding a
  full paragraph of real regulatory stakes (the $50K/day penalty, the 2032 mandate, SB 54 becoming
  the de facto national floor) *before* talking about the product, so the elevated claim ("source
  reduction stops being a sustainability aspiration and becomes a legal requirement") has
  something concrete underneath it. Guide §6, item 9: a lofty closer only works when it's the most
  specific sentence available, not the vaguest.
- **The leadership/moat answer fixes a real AI-tell mid-revision.** The original had a double
  em-dash parenthetical ("one metric in isolation — fees, or recyclability, or footprint —
  forcing teams...") that got converted to a colon-plus-list. Left in this file as a reminder that
  even internal/leadership-facing narrative content gets the same em-dash discipline as
  customer-facing copy.
- **The metrics/proof answer was reordered specifically to fix a feedback note**, not just
  wordsmithed: "save time and save money" was named as the primary business outcome, and hitting
  compliance deadlines was demoted to table stakes ("Meeting deadlines is table stakes, and we
  hold that bar, too... That's the floor. The hours and dollars saved above are the real proof.").
  It also shows the guide's preferred proof pattern in practice: an aggregate stat (2,200+
  simulations, 2,800+ component swaps) paired with one named, quantified example (Tillamook,
  ~$100K, a single misclassification) rather than either alone.

**One piece flagged, not fixed, because it arrived pre-approved:** the short company-pitch answer
("Our platform helps reduce compliance headaches while unlocking powerful insights—and empowers
brands to stand out...") has an em dash and a vague, unsupported claim ("powerful insights") of
exactly the kind Guide §5 flags — an abstract benefit with no number or example attached, unlike
the other three answers in this same submission. It's included here verbatim because it was
submitted as approved, not because it's the standard to match. If this pitch gets reused or
revised elsewhere, tighten it to the same bar as the rest of this submission rather than copying
it as-is.

## The answers

### Company description (Industry: Software)

rePurpose is the leading Packaging Sustainability & Compliance platform for consumer companies. We enable companies to easily measure, report, and act on their plastic footprint.

Our platform helps reduce compliance headaches while unlocking powerful insights—and empowers brands to stand out with Verified Plastic Recovery claims that customers trust.

### Why the Packaging Simulator is a win (product story)

Over the past 12 months, rePurpose built the Packaging Simulator, a tool that lets brands model any packaging change and see its compliance impact before spending a dollar.

California's SB 54 is the first US regulation of its scale to require producers to reduce plastic at the source, not just fund recycling for what's already made. It carries real teeth: penalties up to $50,000 a day, and a 2032 mandate that all packaging be recyclable or compostable. Because few brands manufacture state-specific packaging, SB 54 is becoming the de facto national floor for packaging design.

We saw that shift coming. Customer interviews showed teams modeling trade-offs by hand in spreadsheets, or paying consultants for months, risking a misclassified material or a missed threshold. Every week lost to that friction is a week a better packaging decision doesn't ship.

The Packaging Simulator models fees, recyclability, source-reduction credit, and material footprint side by side for any proposed change, rolled up across a full portfolio, using data we already have for existing clients. A multi-week exercise becomes a same-day decision.

The result: brands can move fast on packaging that reduces plastic at the source, and stay provably compliant while they do it.

### Why the Packaging Simulator is a win (leadership/market positioning)

The Packaging Simulator is a win because it moves rePurpose from a compliance reporting company to a decision-support platform. Our regulatory credibility now extends into forward-looking packaging strategy, not just backward-looking filings.

For our leadership team, it deepens the moat: our 500+ existing clients already have their packaging data with us, so the Simulator launches with zero onboarding friction. This is a structural advantage no new entrant can replicate without years of client trust and data already in hand.

For the industry, it solves a problem no competitor has addressed well because most tools model one metric at a time, including fees, recyclability, or footprint, forcing teams into slow, manual spreadsheet work across each dimension separately. We show every trade-off simultaneously, at both the SKU and portfolio level, so a single packaging decision can be evaluated for its full ripple effect before it's made.

It also positions rePurpose ahead of the market. As California's 2032 recyclability mandates approach and more states adopt EPR laws, brands need a way to plan years ahead, not just react to this year's fee schedule. We built that tool before the demand fully materialized, and before competitors recognized source reduction as a real pain point.

### How we know it's working (metrics and proof)

The clearest proof is in time and money saved. Every state report brings roughly 200+ hours of data-gathering and cross-checking, and packaging changes used to take months to model by hand: fee impact, recyclability, and footprint compared one spreadsheet at a time. rePurpose's compliance platform processed thousands of reports in 2026, while the Packaging Simulator has enabled over 2,200 simulations and 2,800 component swaps since its launch, enabling brands to instantly model packaging changes and save months of work.

The money case is just as direct. Material classification errors can swing a fee dramatically in the wrong direction, and getting it right works the same way in reverse. We saved Tillamook about $100,000 in Oregon fees alone by fixing a single misclassification, and that's not an outlier: one client's fees dropped by up to 91% once their materials were correctly classified. That's real money recovered from getting the classification right the first time.

Meeting deadlines is table stakes, and we hold that bar, too, with 100% on-time submission across every EPR report and Individual Source Reduction Plan filed in the past 12 months, and zero missed deadlines. That's the floor. The hours and dollars saved above are the real proof.
