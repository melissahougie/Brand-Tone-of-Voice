## Version A: Longer with stronger push

**Subject:** Your Oregon fees might be higher than they need to be

Hi [First name],

We've been looking at Oregon EPR fees across our customers, and yours stood out. You're paying well into six figures annually, and a chunk of that may be reducible.

Oregon's eco-modulation program has two ways to lower your fees through a third-party-reviewed Life Cycle Assessment (called a Life Cycle Evaluation, or LCE, in CAA documentation):

- Bonus A rewards measuring and disclosing your packaging's environmental performance, cutting base fees by up to 10% (max $200K).
- Bonus B rewards demonstrating packaging improvements, with reductions that scale by how much impact you cut: up to 20%, 25%, or 30% per report depending on the reduction achieved (capped at $40K, $45K, or $50K per report). Across a portfolio of SKU batches, those add up.
- The assessment has to cover the full range of metrics Oregon scores, including acidification, eutrophication, and plastic leakage, not carbon alone.

The catch is the LCA itself. They take time and need third-party review. Most brands either don't know the bonus exists or assume the assessment costs more than it returns. For a brand paying what you're paying, the LCA runs well below the fees it can unlock.

The other thing worth knowing: that same LCA keeps working after Oregon. It feeds your Scope 3 reporting, answers the product-level sustainability data retailers increasingly ask for, and informs smarter packaging decisions in R&D before you commit to a format.

We work with a partner, Planet FWD, who builds these LCAs specifically for this. ISO-compliant, third-party reviewed, in 6-8 weeks, at roughly half what most consultants charge. We've seen their work and trust it, which is why we're making the connection.

If you'd like an introduction, just reply to this email and we'll set it up.

## Version B: Softer

**Subject:** A way to bring your Oregon fees down

Hi [First name],

Oregon EPR compliance is a real cost, and we know it lands harder on packaging-intensive brands like yours. So when we find a way to take some of that weight off, we want to pass it along.

Oregon's program has a part called eco-modulation. If you can show your packaging's environmental performance through a third-party-reviewed Life Cycle Assessment (called a Life Cycle Evaluation, or LCE, in CAA documentation), you can reduce your base fees two ways:

Bonus A for measuring and disclosing your impact, and Bonus B for demonstrating packaging improvements, which scales with how much you cut. For brands paying what you're paying, it can be worth a look.

And the assessment earns its value beyond Oregon. The same data supports your Scope 3 reporting, answers the sustainability data requests retailers are increasingly sending, and helps your team make smarter packaging choices in R&D.

The LCA is the piece that takes some doing, and it's time-sensitive for the next cycle. We work with a partner, Planet FWD, who handles this kind of assessment for consumer brands, and we trust their work. We'd be glad to introduce you if it's useful.
