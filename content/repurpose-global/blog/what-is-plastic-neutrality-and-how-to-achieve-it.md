## What Is Plastic Neutrality?

[PARAPHRASED — not verbatim, reconstructed from search summaries]
Plastic neutrality means having a net-zero plastic footprint, for a defined period of time. Plastic neutrality is achieved when an individual or organization's plastic footprint is measured and balanced by the removal and recovery of plastic waste from nature, and complemented by reductions in plastic use.

## How to Achieve It

[PARAPHRASED — not verbatim, reconstructed from search summaries]
According to rePurpose Global, there are three steps required for brands (or individuals) going Plastic Neutral: Measure your plastic footprint. Reduce plastic use, and commit to further reductions. Balance your plastic footprint by funding action that recovers as much additional plastic as is being used.

**Step 1 — Measure:** Leading certifying organizations will have an easy-to-use, proprietary assessment tool to calculate plastic footprints.

**Step 2 — Reduce:** This should be achieved through ambitious commitments and progress on reducing virgin plastic use — reducing the size of a plastic footprint as far as possible.

**Step 3 — Balance:** The brand funds the removal and recovery of "additional" plastic waste from nature — to balance their plastic footprint. Plastic waste collection and recovery projects are usually located in plastic waste "hotspots" in places such as India, Indonesia, The Philippines, Ghana and Kenya.

A number of organizations — including rePurpose Global, Plastic Collective and Plastic Bank — are offering "Plastic Neutral" (and "Plastic Negative") certifications.
