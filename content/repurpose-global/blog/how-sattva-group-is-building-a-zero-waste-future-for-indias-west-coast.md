## Overview

[PARAPHRASED — not verbatim, reconstructed from search summaries of third-party CSR news coverage, NOT the rePurpose.global blog post]

The Karnataka Zero-Waste Initiative is a three-year, long-term effort led by Sattva Group and Knowledge Realty Trust in partnership with rePurpose Global to address plastic pollution at its source. Karnataka's coastline stretches for 320 kms through some of India's most stunning shoreline, where plastic accounts for nearly 60 percent of coastal litter.

## Key Goals and Targets

Launched on Earth Day 2026 in partnership with Knowledge Realty Trust, the initiative is a three-year commitment to recover 3.5–4.5 million kilograms of waste. It will bring 80,000 households into formal waste management systems, some of them for the very first time, reduce plastic leakage across 50 coastal villages, and support 200 waste workers, predominantly women, with dignified livelihoods and skills for the future.

## Current Progress

The numbers from the initiative's first months: over 1.5 lakh kilograms of dry waste recovered, more than 50,000 kilograms of low-value plastic ethically processed, 20 waste workers now supported, and active engagement with 30+ village gram panchayats with critical backing from the Dakshina Kannada Zila Panchayat and Mangala Resource Management.

## Goals for End of 2026

By the end of 2026, the Karnataka Zero Waste Initiative aims to recover over 10 lakh kilograms of dry waste including 4 lakh kilograms of plastic, improve livelihoods for 60 waste workers, and run awareness and capacity-building programmes across at least 15 villages in Dakshina Kannada.
