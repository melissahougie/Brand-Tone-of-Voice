## Key Best Practices for Plastic Credits

[PARAPHRASED — not verbatim, reconstructed from search summaries]

**Focus on Low-Value Plastics**: High-value plastics like PET are not ideal for offsetting since they would likely be recycled anyway, whereas effective plastic credits focus on low-value plastic by paying informal workers to intercept it before it reaches oceans or landfills.

**Additionality**: Only plastic waste that would not have been collected without intervention and would have entered the natural environment should be counted towards credits.

**Verification and Transparency**: rePurpose's protocol ensures timely audits, spot checks, unannounced site visits, and impact verification from third-party auditors on an ongoing basis.

**Responsible End-of-Life Management**: Collected plastic that can be recycled should be recycled, while remaining materials go to environmentally-friendly end-of-life solutions such as reprocessing or co-processing.

**Socioeconomic Impact**: Socioeconomic impact should be a best practice rather than just a co-benefit, with plastic credit growth requiring essential focus on fair wages and support for informal waste workers.

**Local Partnership**: Working through vetted local waste management partners provides established expertise, boosts cost-effectiveness, and strengthens overall impact through collaboration rather than competition.
