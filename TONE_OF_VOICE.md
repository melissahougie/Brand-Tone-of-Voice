## 1. The brand in one paragraph

rePurpose Global sells two things that don't usually sit in the same sentence: dry regulatory compliance (EPR filings, state deadlines, fee forecasts) and emotionally resonant environmental impact (waste workers, ocean plastic, whale breeding grounds). The voice's core trick is holding both without sounding schizophrenic — it's a **confident expert who also happens to care**. Never just a vendor, never just an activist.

## 2. Two registers, one brand

The content shows the brand voice operating in two distinct registers depending on audience and channel. Both are legitimate — use the table to pick the right one, don't blend them into mush.

| | **Compliance Register** (homepage, product pages, FAQs, most 2026 blog) | **Impact/Mission Register** (About, Impact, case studies, legacy business site) |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Compliance/ops/brand lead under deadline pressure | Sustainability lead, brand marketer, values-driven buyer |
| Job to be done | Make a scary, technical problem feel solvable | Make an abstract global problem feel personal and winnable |
| Sentence feel | Short, declarative, businesslike. "Report across every EPR state. Forecast fees & model source reductions. Pay one flat price." | Warmer, more cadenced. "A movement of purposeful people and companies going Plastic Neutral to reduce waste, revive lives, and restore nature's balance." |
| Proof style | Dollars, days, percentages: "$100,000 saved," "Cut EPR fees by up to 91%," "$25,000 per day, per violation" | Pounds/kilograms of plastic, people, places: "13,352,592 lbs of plastic waste removed," "174 waste workers formally employed" |
| Emotional register | Urgency + relief ("done faster, done right, done for you") | Dignity + hope ("deserve dignity, fair wages, and safety") |
| Verb energy | Streamline, navigate, forecast, automate, cut, avoid | Restore, revive, protect, empower, fund |

Most current homepage/product copy sits in the Compliance Register with Impact Register woven in as supporting proof (case studies, impact-project stats). When in doubt for new marketing copy aimed at a compliance/ops buyer, default to Compliance Register with one Impact Register beat near the close.

## 3. Personality: five traits, with receipts

**1. Plainspoken translator, not a regulator.** The brand's job is to make dense law legible. It explains jargon the moment it uses it, and isn't afraid of a sentence like: *"Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) is an environmental regulatory framework that makes brands responsible for the packaging they put into the world."* — definition first, no throat-clearing.

**2. Empathetic about the reader's anxiety, without wallowing in it.** From the Oregon blog post: *"Honestly, I get it. EPR went from 'a thing sustainability people talk about at conferences' to 'active enforcement with real penalties' surprisingly fast. But saying 'it's complicated' doesn't help anyone, so let's get more specific."* Names the feeling, then immediately moves to usefulness.

**3. Direct — will say the uncomfortable thing plainly.** *"Let's just say it plainly. Some brands made a calculated bet that enforcement would be slow and the downside of waiting was low. Oregon just answered that question."* No hedging, no corporate euphemism for "you gambled and lost."

**4. Confident without overclaiming — "credible" is a load-bearing word.** Repeated phrase across pages: *"credible sustainability claims... without greenwashing."* The brand actively positions against inflated environmental marketing, which sets a real constraint: claims must always be traceable to a number and a verification method, not a vibe.

**5. Structured optimist.** Even the scariest content (fines, enforcement letters, public non-compliance lists) always resolves into a numbered path forward. The Oregon post's "What to actually do right now" section is the template: name the fear, quantify it, then hand over 5 concrete, sequenced actions.

## 4. Sentence and rhetorical patterns

Patterns observed repeatedly enough across independent pages to treat as house style:

- **Fragment-as-punctuation.** "Done faster, done right, done for you." / "No cost. No obligation." Short fragments close out a thought for emphasis — use sparingly, at the end of a section, not mid-argument.
- **The rhetorical "what if" / scenario question, answered in the same breath.** "A brand based in Georgia selling into Oregon grocery chains? Obligated. A DTC company shipping nationally? Almost certainly obligated in multiple states." Sets up a mini-scenario as a question, answers it in one word or short clause.
- **Arrow (→) to connect a requirement to its implication.** Used repeatedly in bulleted lists: "Annual Supply Reporting (due May 31, 2026) using 2025 data → Requires detailed material reports across 62 material categories." The arrow means "here's what that actually means for you."
- **Objection-in-the-reader's-own-voice, then a reframe.** The FAQ/objection-handling sections quote the anxious version of the question verbatim ("I need to get buy-in and budget approval from our internal stakeholders... EPR is new territory for us.") before answering — never a sanitized paraphrase of the objection.
- **Second person, always.** "You still have to report your supply data." Brand voice rarely hides behind "companies" or "producers" when addressing the reader directly, even though "producer" is the correct legal term used elsewhere.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** Not "many brands" — "300+ brands," "500+ Consumer Companies," "45+ Packaging Regulations." Not "significant plastic recovered" — "191,615 lbs of plastic recovered, equivalent to over 206 million straws, 4.8 million bottles, and 14.4 million shopping bags." Every claim of scale gets a concrete unit and, where possible, a relatable equivalence.
- **Named, titled human proof.** Testimonials always carry a full name, title, and company ("Steve Marko, Senior Director of R&D, Tillamook") — never anonymized ("a leading brand told us...").
- **Three-step reduction.** Complex processes are almost always compressed to exactly three steps (Assess/Compile/Submit; Fund/Track/Communicate; Measure/Fund/Commit) even when the real process has more moving parts. This is a formatting convention worth preserving — three feels manageable, five feels like homework.

## 5. Vocabulary

**House terms — use consistently, don't synonym-swap:**
- "Plastic Neutral" / "Plastic Negative" (capitalized as proper certification names)
- "Plastic Recovery Claims," "Verified Plastic Recovery (VPR)," "plastic credit(s)"
- "EPR" (spelled out once on first use per page, then abbreviated)
- "ISR Plan" (Individual Source Reduction Plan)
- "streamline," "navigate," "forecast," "done for you"
- "credible" (paired almost every time with "without greenwashing")
- "waste workers" (never "waste pickers" or "informal labor" — dignity-preserving term choice is deliberate)

**Avoid:**
- Vague eco-marketing filler: "eco-friendly," "green," "sustainable" used alone with no metric attached. The brand's whole positioning is claims backed by numbers — an unsupported adjective undercuts it.
- Legal certainty the brand doesn't have. Compliance content consistently hedges with "Each producer must determine obligations with their own legal counsel" / "this is not legal advice" whenever a question strays into a genuinely ambiguous legal area — don't state legal conclusions as fact.
- Corporate throat-clearing ("In today's ever-changing regulatory landscape..."). Posts open with a scenario or a direct claim, not scene-setting.
- Passive voice for brand actions. Not "reports were filed" — "we filed your report" / "rePurpose Global centralizes your supply data."

## 6. Structural conventions

- **Hero pattern:** Category claim ("#1 Packaging Compliance Platform") → one-line value prop → 3 short benefit bullets → primary CTA. Repeats near-identically across Homepage, Compliance, and Plastic Recovery Claims pages.
- **CTA vocabulary is role-specific, not generic "Learn More" everywhere:** "Book a Demo" (bottom-of-page, high-intent), "Talk to a Compliance Specialist" (mid-page, consultative), "Get Started" (top-of-page, low-commitment), "Read Case Study" / "Read Story" (proof content), "Check My EPR Status" / "5-minute survey" (self-qualification tools). Match CTA weight to the reader's position in the page.
- **Low-friction reassurance under high-intent CTAs:** "No cost. No obligation." "To confirm your eligibility, take our 5-minute survey." Every ask to talk to a human is de-risked in the same breath.
- **FAQ sections address the buyer's actual internal objections**, not just product questions — e.g. "I need to get buy-in and budget approval from our internal stakeholders and leadership" is treated as a legitimate FAQ, on par with technical questions.
- **Numbers get bolded/isolated as standalone proof points** in case studies and impact sections (e.g., "581,313 lbs" set apart from body copy), not buried in paragraphs.

## 7. Tone by content type — quick reference

| Content type | Tone | Length/format |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage / product pages | Confident, benefit-first, low jargon | Short hero + 3-bullet benefit blocks + testimonials |
| Compliance blog (deadline/enforcement posts) | Direct, empathetic, plain-English translator, first-person allowed | Long-form, scenario-driven, numbered action lists |
| Webinar/event promo | Punchy, slightly informal, FOMO-adjacent ("Candid answers. No slides. No recording.") | 2-4 sentences, single CTA |
| Case studies | Proof-forward, third person, quantified | Headline stat + 2-3 sentence narrative + CTA |
| FAQ / Q&A | Precise, hedged where legally ambiguous, still conversational | Question verbatim + direct answer, disclaimers where needed |
| About/Mission/Impact | Warmer, values-forward, movement language | Narrative with founder story, framework naming (e.g. "3+1 Planetary Crisis") |

## 8. Before / after

**Generic corporate version:**
> Our platform leverages industry-leading technology to help companies achieve compliance with evolving environmental regulations across multiple jurisdictions, ensuring peace of mind for stakeholders.

**rePurpose voice:**
> EPR compliance – done faster, done right, done for you. Report across every EPR state, forecast your fees, and pay one flat price. Talk to a Compliance Specialist — no cost, no obligation.

**Generic corporate version:**
> We are committed to environmental sustainability and work with partners globally to reduce plastic waste in vulnerable ecosystems.

**rePurpose voice:**
> Partnering with informal waste workers and bringing new collection and recycling systems to protect Indonesia's precious ocean ecosystems. 13,352,592 lbs of plastic waste removed. 174 waste workers formally employed.

## 9. Quick checklist for anyone writing as rePurpose Global

- [ ] Did I name the reader's actual fear/objection before answering it?
- [ ] Does every impact or savings claim have a specific number attached (not "significant" or "many")?
- [ ] Is the CTA the right weight for where this sits on the page (Get Started vs. Talk to a Specialist vs. Book a Demo)?
- [ ] Did I use "credible" language instead of unsupported "green"/"sustainable" adjectives?
- [ ] If this touches legal/regulatory certainty, did I hedge appropriately ("consult your own legal counsel")?
- [ ] Can this be said in fewer, shorter sentences? Fragments are allowed for emphasis.
- [ ] If this is impact/mission content, are the people (waste workers, communities) named and counted, not abstracted?
